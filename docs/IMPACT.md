# Why visibility + gamification reduces the real footprint (IMPACT)

1. **You can't fix what you can't see.** Normalising utility data per person and per square metre
   tells a hostel block or a department whether it — not its neighbours — is the hotspot, so
   attention goes where the waste actually is. Most campuses currently run this on annual bills;
   GreenScore turns it into a monthly behaviour signal.
2. **Social norms beat slogans.** Normalised leaderboards make reducing kWh/person and kg/person
   visible, comparable and competitive between departments and hostels. Seeing a peer block use
   18% less water reframes one's own habits without a lecture.
3. **Streaks and challenges reward the behaviour, not the app.** Points come only from verified
   improvements and sustained logging consistency — so the incentive tracks resource savings,
   not engagement metrics. Anti-gaming rules (no raw-volume points, spikes earn nothing,
   missing data earns nothing) keep the reported win honest.
4. **Auditability protects the gains.** The hash-chained ledger means numbers can't be quietly
   rewritten, which is exactly what accreditation reviewers (NAAC Criterion 7) and students will
   ask about. Trustworthy data makes it safe to publish and to act on.
5. **Report generation removes the scramble.** Turning 24 months of messy readings into a
   structured, reproducible Criterion-7 report is hours of manual work today; when the honest
   report is one click away, the incentive to actually instrument and log goes up.

GreenScore doesn't "green" a campus — it measures honestly, and gives students, staff and
leadership a reason to act on the measurement.
